# MYBIE SWORD — Governance Framework

## 1. Purpose and Status

This framework defines the governance principles, decision rights, accountability expectations, and approval boundaries for MYBIE SWORD.

It is a **foundational governance policy**. It does not claim that a staffed board, community voting system, treasury committee, independent audit function, or formal legal entity currently exists. Such mechanisms must be established and documented before being described as operational.

## 2. Governing Principles

All project decisions should follow:
- **Security:** Protect accounts, assets, systems, and community members.
- **Transparency:** Communicate material decisions and verified developments.
- **Honesty:** Avoid misleading statements and unsupported claims.
- **Responsibility:** Assess consequences before acting.
- **Accountability:** Record who approved material actions and why.
- **Proportionality:** Match controls to the impact and risk of a decision.

Transparency must not expose private credentials, personal data, or security-sensitive information.

## 3. Governance Roles

### Project Founder

The founder establishes the vision and core principles, appoints authorized representatives, and retains responsibility for major strategic decisions unless authority is explicitly delegated.

### Authorized Project Representative

A person may handle specified operational or approval duties only when the founder has explicitly delegated them. Delegation must define scope, limits, duration, and reporting expectations.

### Community and Content Functions

These functions may be introduced as the project develops. Their duties and staffing status are defined in the Community Team Structure. A role title alone does not confer authority.

### Contributors

Contributors may propose and complete approved work within the scope assigned to them. They do not automatically gain authority over project policy, accounts, assets, or public commitments.

## 4. Decision Classes and Approval

| Decision class | Examples | Minimum approval principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine operations | Routine moderation, approved content formatting, documentation corrections | Assigned role owner within documented permissions |
| Public communications | Material announcements, campaign claims, public responses on sensitive matters | Authorized communications approver |
| Strategic decisions | Brand direction, new projects, material changes to public commitments | Founder or explicitly delegated authority |
| Financial decisions | Spending, compensation, paid promotion, partnership payments | Founder or specifically authorized financial approver; documented record |
| Token and technical decisions | Launch configuration, token authorities, smart-contract changes | Authorized technical decision-maker and explicit project approval; independent technical review where feasible |
| Security-critical decisions | Wallet access, account recovery, suspected compromise, permission changes | Authorized security owner; emergency actions documented and reviewed |

No one may approve an action outside their delegated authority. Where one person currently holds multiple roles, material financial or security-sensitive actions should receive an additional independent review whenever feasible.

## 5. Delegation and Access

Every delegation should identify:
- The person receiving authority.
- The specific task and permitted actions.
- The systems or information accessible.
- The approval limits and escalation route.
- The start, review, and end conditions.

Use least privilege: grant only the minimum access necessary, for only as long as necessary. Wallet signing authority must never be bundled with ordinary community or marketing permissions.

## 6. Financial and Treasury Governance

This framework does not assert that a project treasury or multi-person approval system is currently active.

Before project funds are held or spent, a separate policy should define:
- Official wallet addresses and their purpose.
- Who controls each wallet and under what authority.
- Spending limits and approval requirements.
- Records of deposits, withdrawals, fees, and payments.
- Reconciliation and public reporting expectations.
- Emergency response and access-revocation procedures.

Never publish seed phrases or private keys. Public wallet addresses may be disclosed when doing so is safe and appropriate; disclosure must not expose access credentials.

## 7. Token and Distribution Transparency

Public token commitments must match the actual launch configuration and on-chain activity. Before making claims, verify the relevant settings and evidence.

Where applicable, publish the token mint address, relevant authority status, distribution method, creator/project wallet disclosures, and links to public blockchain records. Clearly distinguish facts, intentions, and commitments. Correct inaccurate statements promptly and preserve a traceable record of material corrections.

Do not describe a distribution commitment as independently verified unless evidence supports that description.

## 8. Conflicts of Interest

Anyone acting for the project must disclose material financial interests, paid promotions, referral benefits, personal relationships, or other conflicts relevant to a decision.

A conflicted person should not be the sole approver of the related transaction or public claim where an alternative reviewer is available. Paid promotions and partnerships must be accurately disclosed.

## 9. Records, Reporting, and Accountability

Maintain appropriate records for material decisions, including:
- Date and subject.
- Decision owner and approver.
- Relevant rationale and risks.
- Financial impact, if any.
- Implementation status and supporting evidence.

Do not store passwords, private keys, seed phrases, recovery codes, or unnecessary personal data in governance records or the repository.

When an incident or failure occurs:
1. Contain immediate harm.
2. Identify the responsible owner.
3. Assess impact and preserve relevant evidence safely.
4. Take corrective action.
5. Communicate material facts without exposing sensitive information.
6. Record lessons and follow-up actions.

## 10. Community Feedback and Disputes

Community members may raise questions, criticism, and proposals through official channels. Good-faith criticism must not be treated as misconduct solely because it is critical.

Moderation disputes should follow the appeal process in the Community Guidelines. Community polls or informal feedback are advisory unless a formal voting mechanism, scope, eligibility rules, and binding effect have been separately published.

## 11. Review and Amendment

Review this framework when the project introduces new roles, holds project funds, launches a token, changes critical account access, or materially expands operations.

Amendments should be committed to the repository with a clear change history and communicated through official channels when material.

## 12. Limitations

This document is an internal project governance framework, not legal advice, an audit report, a guarantee of security, or proof that every control is implemented. Legal, tax, regulatory, and technical questions should be reviewed by appropriately qualified professionals when necessary.

## 13. Final Statement

Authority must come with responsibility. Decisions must have clear owners. Trust must be supported by evidence and consistent action.
