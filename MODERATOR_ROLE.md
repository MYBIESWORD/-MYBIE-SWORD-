# ⚔️ MYBIE SWORD — Moderator Role and Operating Rules

## 1. Status and Purpose

This document defines the moderator role for use **when a moderator is formally appointed**. It does not mean a moderator team is currently staffed or active.

A moderator is trusted to help protect the community—not to control project funds, make strategic decisions, or speak beyond assigned authority.

## 2. Appointment and Authorization

Before performing moderator duties, a person must receive:
- Explicit appointment from the authorized project representative.
- A written description of responsibilities and escalation contacts.
- Training on the Community Guidelines and security procedures.
- Only the account permissions needed for the assigned work.

Moderator status does not automatically grant access to the official X account, email, repository administration, wallet, or treasury.

## 3. Core Responsibilities

When appointed and authorized, moderators may:
- Welcome members and answer routine questions using verified public information.
- Keep discussions respectful and relevant.
- Apply the Community Guidelines consistently.
- Remove spam, phishing, malicious links, and harmful content within assigned permissions.
- Identify impersonation and suspicious activity.
- Share approved announcements without changing their meaning.
- Record and escalate significant incidents, complaints, and recurring questions.

## 4. Official Account and Announcement Controls

Moderators may publish from an official account only when that specific access has been explicitly granted.

Before publishing:
1. Confirm the information against an approved source.
2. Obtain required approval for material announcements.
3. Check links, spelling, dates, and claims.
4. Do not add unapproved promises, token-price claims, partnership claims, or launch details.
5. Preserve a record of material announcements where appropriate.

Do not share login credentials with another person. Use individual access and multi-factor authentication where supported. If access is suspected to be compromised, stop using the account and immediately escalate the incident.

## 5. Security Rules

Moderators must never:
- Ask for, receive, store, or transmit a member's seed phrase or private key.
- Request passwords, recovery codes, or one-time authentication codes.
- Access or control project wallets or treasury funds.
- Ask members to connect wallets or sign transactions as a condition of support.
- Install unknown files or connect official accounts to unverified applications.

Moderators should warn members about suspicious links and direct them to verified official resources. A moderator must not claim to have recovered or secured a member's wallet.

## 6. Communication Standards

Moderators must:
- Be clear, respectful, calm, and factual.
- Distinguish verified project information from personal opinion.
- Admit when they do not know an answer and escalate it.
- Never promise profits, guaranteed returns, token prices, exchange listings, or future outcomes.
- Never provide personalized investment, legal, or tax advice.
- Avoid arguing with critics or retaliating against good-faith questions.

## 7. Authority and Prohibited Actions

A moderator may act only within the permissions assigned to them.

Without separate explicit authorization, moderators may not:
- Make financial or strategic decisions.
- Change tokenomics, mint settings, smart contracts, or project configuration.
- Spend project funds or solicit donations.
- Negotiate or confirm partnerships.
- Make binding commitments on behalf of the project.
- Appoint other moderators or grant account access.
- Publish confidential information or unverified claims.

## 8. Moderation Actions and Appeals

Moderators should apply proportionate actions under the Community Guidelines. For serious or urgent threats, they may take immediate protective action within their permissions and report it promptly.

Where practical, record the reason, action, date, and relevant evidence without collecting unnecessary personal data. Members may request a review through an official channel. An appeal should be reviewed by an authorized person not directly involved in the original decision where practical.

## 9. Incident Escalation

Escalate immediately if there is:
- A suspected compromise of an official account or project system.
- A credible phishing, wallet-draining, malware, or impersonation campaign.
- A request for wallet credentials or unauthorized transfer of funds.
- A threat to a community member.
- An unauthorized announcement or suspected misuse of project authority.

Do not investigate beyond your assigned capability, confront suspected attackers, or publish sensitive incident details.

## 10. Conduct, Conflicts, and Offboarding

Moderators must act with integrity, fairness, confidentiality, and accountability. Disclose relevant conflicts of interest and do not use the role for personal financial gain.

When a moderator leaves or is removed:
- Revoke account and tool access promptly.
- Rotate any shared secrets that may have been exposed.
- Transfer outstanding reports and project-owned materials securely.
- Confirm that no project credentials or private information remain in personal possession.

## 11. Final Statement

Being a moderator is a responsibility, not merely a title. Authority must remain limited, transparent, and accountable.
